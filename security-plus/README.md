## Overview

CompTIA Security+ is a vendor-neutral cybersecurity certification designed to build a broad foundation across the major areas of information security.

The SY0-701 exam covers areas such as:

- General security concepts
- Threats, vulnerabilities, and mitigations
- Security architecture
- Security operations
- Identity and access management
- Network security
- Incident response
- Risk management
- Governance and compliance

Security+ was my first professional cybersecurity certification.

When I started preparing for it, I had no previous cybersecurity work experience and no practical cybersecurity background.

Because of that, Security+ became the certification that gave me the foundation for almost everything I studied afterward.

I completed the SY0-701 version on **March 18, 2026** at a physical testing center.

After reading the book, I used Andrew Ramdayal's course to reinforce the material from a different perspective.

### Andrew Ramdayal Practice Exams

I used Andrew Ramdayal's practice exams to test whether I actually understood the material.

They helped expose areas where I recognized the terminology but did not fully understand how to apply the concept.

---

### Jason Dion Practice Exams

I also completed Jason Dion's Security+ practice exams.

Using practice exams from more than one source exposed me to different wording and scenario styles.

The important part for me was not memorizing the answers.

Whenever I answered something incorrectly, I tried to understand:

- Why was my answer wrong?
- Why was the correct answer better?
- Which security principle was being tested?
- What detail in the scenario changed the answer?
- What change could make another option correct?

That made the practice exams part of the learning process rather than simply a score check.

## Understanding Was More Important Than Memorization

One of the strongest lessons from Security+ was that knowing a definition is not the same as understanding the concept.

For example, memorizing:

```text
MFA = Multi-Factor Authentication
```

is useful.

But understanding MFA means being able to answer questions such as:

- Why is MFA used?
- What problem does it solve?
- What qualifies as a different authentication factor?
- Against which attacks can it help?
- What are its limitations?

The same principle applies to:

- Firewalls
- Encryption
- Authentication
- Network segmentation
- Access control
- Vulnerability management
- Incident response
- Risk controls

Scenario-based questions become much easier once you understand **why** a technology or control exists.

---

## Theory vs. Performance-Based Questions

One thing I noticed during the actual exam was that I did not need to separately memorize solutions for the Performance-Based Questions.

If I understood the theory properly, I could apply it to the practical scenario.

My experience was roughly:

```text
Understand the Theory
        ↓
Understand What the Control Does
        ↓
Understand the Scenario
        ↓
Identify What the Question Is Asking
        ↓
Apply the Correct Concept
```

For me, the PBQs did not feel technically difficult once the underlying concepts were clear.

They were not advanced hands-on labs.

They required applying concepts I had already studied.

For example, knowing that a firewall exists is not enough.

You should understand:

- What traffic should be allowed?
- What traffic should be blocked?
- Which direction is the traffic moving?
- Which protocol is being used?
- Why is the rule needed?

Once the concept makes sense, applying it to a simple practical scenario becomes much easier.

## My Exam Experience

I took Security+ in person at a testing center.

It was my first professional cybersecurity certification exam.

Because I had spent the previous two weeks studying intensively, my main focus during the exam was carefully reading each scenario and identifying exactly what the question was asking.

Security+ questions can include details that are not equally important.

Words such as:

- **BEST**
- **MOST secure**
- **FIRST**
- **PRIMARY**
- **MOST likely**

can significantly change the correct answer.

One useful habit was separating:

```text
Scenario Details
        ↓
Relevant Information
        ↓
What Is Actually Being Asked?
```

That helped me avoid choosing an answer that was technically valid but not the best answer for the specific scenario.

This was probably the clearest benefit I noticed afterward.

Security+ gave me the theory.

For example:

| What is incident response? | Investigate and respond to incidents |
| What is phishing? | Analyze a phishing email |
| What is hashing? | Use hashes during investigations |
| What is a security control? | Understand how controls affect an incident |

Another way I think about the transition is:

```text
Security+

"What is incident response?"

"What is phishing?"

"What is hashing?"

        ↓

"Use the SIEM."

"Investigate the IoC."

"Analyze the phishing email."

"Examine the forensic evidence."

"Reconstruct the incident."
```

Because Security+ had already introduced the terminology and underlying concepts, I could focus much more heavily on practical investigation skills when I started BTL1.

## Important Things to Know Before Starting

### Security+ Is Broad, Not Deep

Security+ covers many cybersecurity topics, but it does not make you an expert in each one.

For example:

```text
Security+:
"What does a SIEM do?"
```

does not equal:

```text
Experienced SIEM Analyst
```

Similarly:

```text
Security+:
"What is SQL injection?"
```

does not equal:

```text
Penetration Tester
```

That breadth is part of the certification's purpose.

---

### Practice Exams Should Be Learning Tools

A high practice-exam score does not automatically mean you understand the material.

The useful part is reviewing:

- Why the correct answer is correct
- Why your answer was wrong
- Why the other options are less appropriate
- Which detail in the scenario matters

I used both Andrew Ramdayal and Jason Dion practice exams for this reason.

---

### You Can Start Without Cybersecurity Experience

I did.

Security+ was my first professional cybersecurity certification, and I started without previous cybersecurity work experience.

However, someone starting from zero should expect the preparation process to involve learning many concepts at the same time.
